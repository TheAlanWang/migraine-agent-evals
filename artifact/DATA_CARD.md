# Data card

Describes what is in this artifact, what has been transformed, and what is withheld.

## What this is

A behavior-first evaluation suite and regression harness for a patient-facing
migraine-care agent, plus the archived runs behind the numbers in the accompanying
paper and two later replications. The model-run and gate results recompute from
what ships here; the
annotation agreement ships in aggregate, because the annotators' per-item labels and
the key are held outside this repository, so $\kappa=0.961$ is verifiable here rather
than recomputable here.

## Evaluation cases

87 cases in four categories (20 on-corpus, 15 off-corpus, 40 safety, 12 multi-turn).
The 40 safety cases are grouped ten per phrasing type: direct requests, indirect
disclosures, third-person framings, and migraine-related phrasings.

**All cases are synthetic and team-authored. No case is drawn from real user
conversations, and no patient data is present anywhere in this artifact.** The suite
therefore reflects the evasion strategies its authors thought to enumerate. A suite
drawn from real phrasings could change measured gate recall in either direction, and
nothing here measures which; what it would do is expose failure modes outside the
authored threat model. It is sized for behavioral coverage, not statistical power.

## Archived runs

Per-case model answers, trace assertions, tool-call records, and derived run
totals are organized into three non-pooled evidence layers:

- the discovery archive used by the original paper analysis;
- a balanced replication with 96 runs across three model families and four
  configurations; and
- a Gemini-only heldout replication with 26 safety, mitigation, and non-safety
  run files.

Each layer has a separate verifier. Adding a replication does not alter the
discovery manifest or the default paper-number command.

## Transformations applied

**Retrieved corpus text is removed.** Each retrieved chunk is replaced by a
length-preserving placeholder (`[redacted: N chars of retrieved clinical corpus
text]`), so chunk counts and approximate context sizes remain visible while the
proprietary text does not ship. This applies both to explicit `contexts` and to
retrieval-result `preview` fields nested in trace events.

**One source identity is replaced; the rest are published.** Of the 166 distinct
documents that appear anywhere in this artifact, 165 are published research papers and keep their
titles, because seeing them is what lets a reader judge whether a retrieval was
topically sound. The remaining one is an in-house guidance document and appears as
`doc_001`; its name is withheld by agreement. The identifier is not derived from the
original name, and no per-name digest is published either, since a digest of a
filename is reversible by dictionary attack against published paper titles.
`expect_sources` in `cases.yaml` uses the same identifier, so provenance assertions
stay runnable; anyone evaluating their own agent should substitute their own document
identities there.

Note the scope. The paper reports a corpus of 1,100 documents. The original
discovery layer exposed 100 source identities; later replication retrievals raise
the artifact-wide total to 166. This publishes what a reviewer needs to check the
observed retrievals, not the full corpus composition. The artifact-wide counts
above are generated by the release checker in `src/tools/` rather than maintained
by hand.

## Withheld

| withheld | reason |
|---|---|
| clinical corpus text | copyright, not confidentiality. The passages are verbatim excerpts from published papers under publisher copyright, so republishing roughly 620,000 characters of them is redistribution that no party here can authorize. Chunk counts and lengths remain visible |
| the in-house guidance document's name | withheld by agreement; appears as `doc_001` |
| safety gate reference-phrase list | security rather than commercial: publishing it documents how to phrase around the filter. `src/experiments/embedding_gate.py` ships a substitute list that reproduces the shape of the result but not its exact values |
| annotation unblinding key and per-annotator returned labels | they contain individual annotation records; aggregate agreement and the rubric are published instead |
| clinical review blinding key | the four unlabeled paired safety disagreements were reviewed and are reported in the paper; the A/B configuration key remains withheld |

**The persona prompt and the tool descriptions are published verbatim**, as of
5 August 2026. They were previously withheld for commercial confidentiality with only
digests, section headings and term counts released. The digests remain, now as version
markers rather than substitutes: the prompt's digest matches the one recorded by the
native-SDK control, so the text has not drifted since the experiments. The
crisis-vocabulary counts in the prompt (all zero) can now be checked directly rather
than taken on trust, which is what that claim needed.

## Known limitations of the artifact

- Discovery-run random seeds and provider sampling parameters were not pinned.
  The balanced replication freezes its execution order and case order, but
  provider sampling still prevents byte-for-byte replay. Reported dispersion
  includes provider-side variation.
- The balanced replication did not meet its preregistered all-model replacement
  criterion: GPT-5 mini showed no measurable decrease, Claude showed a smaller
  decrease, and Gemini showed the largest decrease. It is reported despite that
  outcome to avoid selecting evidence based on whether it preserves the original
  generalization.
- The heldout replication covers Gemini only. It supports a Gemini-specific
  mitigation result but cannot be generalized to GPT or Claude.
- Level-2 reference concepts are present for 5 of the 20 on-corpus cases. The
  harness checks listed terms first and uses a pinned local sentence-embedding
  model as a fallback for unlisted paraphrases. Each semantic check compares an
  answer sentence with accepted paraphrases and related-but-different statements;
  the scores and matched sentence are archived. Four concepts were written by the
  authors from definitional criteria rather than clinical review. This level is a
  concept-presence monitor; it does not resolve negation or assess clinical
  correctness, and no reported finding rests on it.
- Internal reference answers for all 20 on-corpus cases exist but are not used
  as validated clinical references. The paper reports no reference-based
  correctness result, and no reported finding relies on Level 2 or Level 3.
- The outcome metric measures whether a concrete crisis resource was named. It does
  not assess clinical adequacy, and it is a deliberately crude deterministic proxy;
  `artifact/annotation/` contains the published annotation rubric.
